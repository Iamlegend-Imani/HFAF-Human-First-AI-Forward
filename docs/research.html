Research questions

  • When does AI use augment cognition, and when does it substitute for cognition?
  • Which human capabilities become more valuable as generative production becomes cheaper?
  • How should assessment change when artifact production is weak evidence of independent understanding?
  • What forms of AI disclosure meaningfully support academic integrity?
  • Can structured AI critique improve critical-thinking or verification skills?
  • How does repeated cognitive delegation affect skill retention and independent problem-solving?
  • Which aspects of professional and academic judgment should remain nondelegable?
  • How should appropriate AI use differ across disciplines?
  • How do AI-mediated behaviors affect attention, cognition, stress, health, and capacity over time?

Foundational bibliography

UNESCO: Guidance for Generative AI in Education and Research

Miao, F., & Holmes, W. (2023). Guidance for generative AI in education and research. UNESCO.

Relevant because UNESCO frames responsible GenAI adoption through a human-centered approach emphasizing ethical, safe, equitable, meaningful, and pedagogically appropriate use.

Open source →

EDUCAUSE: AI Literacy in Teaching and Learning

Kassorla, M., Georgieva, M., & Papini, A. (2024). AI Literacy in Teaching and Learning: A Durable Framework for Higher Education.

Useful for its emphasis on technical understanding, critical evaluation, practical application, ethical use, and vigilance around misuse and bias.

Open source →

Harvard University: Teach with Generative AI

Generative AI @ Harvard: Teaching Resources.

Relevant to assessment redesign around human learning, creativity, application, critical thinking, process, and demonstrated understanding.

Open source →

EDUCAUSE Review: A Framework for AI Literacy

A Framework for AI Literacy. EDUCAUSE Review (2024).

A neighboring framework that scaffolds AI literacy through understanding, using/applying, analyzing/evaluating, and creating AI.

Faculty Workshop · 60–90 Minutes

What Should Remain Human?

Move the faculty conversation beyond ban AI / embrace AI and toward intentional decisions about human capability, augmentation, assessment, and accountability.

Outcomes

  • Identify the human capability underneath an assignment.
  • Distinguish augmentation from substitution.
  • Choose an appropriate AI-use mode.
  • Redesign one existing assignment.
  • Establish verification and disclosure expectations.
  • Articulate what must remain human in the discipline.

Workshop Flow

1. Opening question, 10 min

What are you afraid humans will lose if AI becomes deeply integrated into education? Capture concerns without immediately debating them. Then ask: What could humans gain?

2. Reframe, 10 min

Introduce the proposition: Technology should increase human capacity without decreasing human agency. Introduce the Agency Loop and Use Spectrum.

3. Assignment autopsy, 15 min

What capability is this actually teaching? What currently demonstrates understanding? Could AI produce the artifact without developing that capability? What should remain human?

4. Redesign, 20 min

Redesign around human outcome, human-only zone, AI role, verification, disclosure, and demonstration of understanding.

5. Adversarial test, 10 min

One participant identifies how AI could circumvent the intended learning. The other strengthens the assignment.

6. Disciplinary boundary, 10 min

Complete: In my discipline, humans must remain responsible for ______ because ______.

7. Closing, 5 min

Name one thing you will prohibit, one thing you will permit, and one thing you will deliberately teach students to do with AI.

This workshop is not designed to convert skeptics into AI enthusiasts. Skepticism can be useful. The objective is to replace reflexive acceptance or rejection with informed human agency.

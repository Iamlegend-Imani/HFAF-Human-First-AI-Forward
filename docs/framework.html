Core Framework

The Human–AI Agency Loop

A five-stage model for using AI to expand capability while preserving human agency.

1. Human Intent

Begin with the human, not the tool. Ask: What am I trying to understand, decide, create, or accomplish? Human intention establishes the objective, stakes, and definition of success.

2. Context

Give the system enough of the world surrounding the problem to collaborate meaningfully: prior thinking, evidence, lived experience, constraints, uncertainties, audience, disciplinary knowledge, values, voice, and competing considerations.

3. Collaboration

Use AI where augmentation creates leverage. Possible roles include research assistant, tutor, critic, simulator, editor, brainstorming partner, translator, coding assistant, synthesis engine, analyst, or question generator.

Ask not only “Can AI do this?” Ask “What division of cognitive labor best serves the human objective?”

4. Discernment

Interrogate the output. Is it true? What evidence supports it? What is missing? What assumptions were introduced? Could bias affect the result? What alternative interpretation exists? Can you defend the answer without appealing to AI as authority?

5. Human Authorship

Return decision rights to the human. What do you believe? What will you keep, change, or reject? What are you willing to defend? What action will you take? What are you accountable for?

Human Intent → Context → AI Collaboration → Human Discernment → Human Authorship → Reflection → renewed Human Intent

This is a loop rather than a pipeline because outcomes generate new information. Humans reflect, update their understanding, and begin another cycle with richer context.

AI should reduce the distance between human intention and human execution without reducing human agency.

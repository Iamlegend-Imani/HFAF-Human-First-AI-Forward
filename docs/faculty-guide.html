Implementation 01

Human First, AI Forward: Faculty Guide

The goal is not to convince every educator to embrace every AI tool. The goal is to give educators enough conceptual clarity to make intentional decisions about where AI belongs, where it does not, and what must remain human.

Start with learning, not technology

  1. What capability is the student supposed to develop?
  2. What evidence would demonstrate that capability?
  3. Which portions require independent human cognition?
  4. Where could AI increase exploration, feedback, accessibility, iteration, or application?
  5. Where could AI undermine the learning objective?
  6. How will students verify AI-generated information?
  7. How will AI use be disclosed?
  8. How will the student demonstrate understanding beyond the submitted artifact?

The central educational challenge

Generative AI complicates the historical relationship between producing an artifact and demonstrating understanding. An essay, program, presentation, image, or analysis can no longer always be treated as sufficient evidence of the cognition that produced it.

This creates an opportunity to strengthen assessment around reasoning, process, application, oral defense, source evaluation, iteration, judgment, and reflection.

Do not default to a binary

Human Only → AI Consulted → AI Assisted → AI Collaborative → AI Evaluated → AI Designed → Human Verified

Questions for faculty resistance

  • What are you afraid students will stop learning?
  • Which intellectual practices are essential to your discipline?
  • What forms of authorship matter in your field?
  • Which risks concern you: hallucination, privacy, bias, dependency, inequity, environmental impact, academic integrity, deskilling?
  • Which risks can be mitigated?
  • Which risks require a human-only boundary?
  • Where might refusing AI exposure itself leave students underprepared?

The objective is not persuasion for its own sake. It is informed agency.

Assignment redesign protocol

1. Name the capabilityAfter this assignment, students should be better able to…
2. Identify the human coreWhat cognitive work must the student perform independently?
3. Identify possible augmentationWhere could AI improve feedback, iteration, comparison, simulation, accessibility, or exploration?
4. Introduce verificationRequire students to check claims, sources, calculations, assumptions, or reasoning.
5. Make process visibleUse process notes, drafts, decision logs, source trails, oral explanation, demonstrations, or reflections.
6. Require authorshipStudents should explain what they accepted, rejected, changed, and ultimately believe.
Do not ask only whether students used AI. Ask what thinking remained theirs.

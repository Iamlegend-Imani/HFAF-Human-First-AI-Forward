# Research Foundation

Human First, AI Forward is intended to develop as a research-informed framework rather than a collection of unsupported claims.

## Research tracks

1. **Human agency and cognitive augmentation**
2. **AI literacy**
3. **Higher-education pedagogy and assessment**
4. **Human–AI collaboration**
5. **Automation, deskilling, and cognitive offloading**
6. **AI ethics, accountability, bias, and governance**
7. **Technology, behavior, cognition, health, and human capacity**
8. **Disciplinary differences in appropriate AI use**

## Research questions

- When does AI use augment cognition, and when does it substitute for cognition?
- Which human capabilities become more valuable as generative production becomes cheaper?
- How should assessment change when artifact production is weak evidence of independent understanding?
- What forms of AI disclosure meaningfully support academic integrity?
- Can structured AI critique improve critical-thinking or verification skills?
- How does repeated cognitive delegation affect skill retention and independent problem-solving?
- Which aspects of professional and academic judgment should remain nondelegable?
- How should appropriate AI use differ across disciplines?
- How do AI-mediated behaviors affect attention, cognition, stress, health, and capacity over time?

## Evidence standard

Claims presented as research findings should be traceable to credible sources. Conceptual propositions developed by Human First, AI Forward should be clearly distinguished from established empirical findings.

The bibliography and literature review will expand as the project develops.

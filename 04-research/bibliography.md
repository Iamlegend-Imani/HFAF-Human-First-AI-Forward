# Research Bibliography

Human First, AI Forward distinguishes between **HFAF conceptual propositions** and **claims grounded in external research or institutional guidance**. This bibliography is the beginning of the project's research spine and should expand as the framework develops.

## Foundational sources

### UNESCO — Guidance for Generative AI in Education and Research

Miao, F., & Holmes, W. (2023). *Guidance for generative AI in education and research*. UNESCO.

https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/guidance-generative-ai-education-and-research

**Why it matters to HFAF:** UNESCO explicitly frames responsible generative-AI adoption through a human-centered approach, including ethical, safe, equitable, meaningful, and pedagogically appropriate use. This supports HFAF's insistence that AI adoption should be designed around human agency and human development rather than technological capability alone.

---

### EDUCAUSE — AI Literacy in Teaching and Learning: A Durable Framework for Higher Education

Kassorla, M., Georgieva, M., & Papini, A. (2024). *AI Literacy in Teaching and Learning: A Durable Framework for Higher Education*. EDUCAUSE.

https://www.educause.edu/content/2024/ai-literacy-in-teaching-and-learning/introduction

**Why it matters to HFAF:** EDUCAUSE defines AI literacy as more than operational prompting. Its framework combines technical understanding, critical evaluation, practical application, ethical use, and ongoing vigilance around bias, misuse, and misapplication. This aligns with the HFAF principle that useful AI collaboration requires discernment rather than passive acceptance of generated output.

### Harvard University — Teach with Generative AI

Harvard University. *Teach with Generative AI — Generative AI @ Harvard.*

https://www.harvard.edu/ai/teaching-resources/

**Why it matters to HFAF:** Harvard's faculty guidance recommends redesigning assessment around human-based learning, creativity, application, critical thinking, and evidence of process rather than relying exclusively on final products that generative AI can easily produce. This directly informs HFAF's question: **What is education for when producing an answer is no longer evidence that someone understands the answer?**

---

### Barnard / EDUCAUSE Review — A Framework for AI Literacy

*A Framework for AI Literacy*. EDUCAUSE Review (2024).

https://er.educause.edu/articles/2024/6/a-framework-for-ai-literacy

**Why it matters to HFAF:** This model scaffolds AI literacy through four levels: understanding AI, using and applying AI, analyzing and evaluating AI, and creating AI. HFAF's Human–AI Use Spectrum is conceptually distinct, but this provides an important neighboring framework for comparison.

## Evidence discipline

A source appearing in this bibliography does **not** mean it validates every HFAF proposition. Sources should be cited only for claims they actually support. HFAF concepts such as the Human–AI Agency Loop, the Human–AI Use Spectrum, and the maxim **Delegate execution. Never delegate agency.** should be identified as original conceptual contributions unless later empirical work specifically validates them.

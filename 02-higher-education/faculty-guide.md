# Human First, AI Forward: Faculty Guide

## The premise

The goal is not to convince every educator to embrace every AI tool.

The goal is to give educators enough conceptual clarity to make intentional decisions about where AI belongs, where it does not, and what must remain human.

## Start with learning, not technology

For every course, assignment, or activity, ask:

1. What capability is the student supposed to develop?
2. What evidence would demonstrate that capability?
3. Which portions of the work require independent human cognition?
4. Where could AI increase exploration, feedback, accessibility, iteration, or application?
5. Where could AI undermine the learning objective?
6. How will students verify AI-generated information?
7. How will AI use be disclosed?
8. How will the student demonstrate understanding beyond the artifact they submit?

## The central educational challenge

Generative AI complicates the historical relationship between **producing an artifact** and **demonstrating understanding**.

An essay, program, presentation, image, or analysis can no longer always be treated as sufficient evidence of the cognition that produced it.

This creates an opportunity to strengthen assessment around reasoning, process, application, oral defense, source evaluation, iteration, judgment, and reflection.

## Do not default to a binary

Use the Human–AI Use Spectrum:

**Human Only → AI Consulted → AI Assisted → AI Collaborative → AI Evaluated → AI Designed → Human Verified**

Different learning objectives can legitimately require different modes.

## Questions for faculty resistance

Resistance may represent legitimate concerns rather than technological ignorance. Surface those concerns explicitly:

- What are you afraid students will stop learning?
- Which intellectual practices are essential to your discipline?
- What forms of authorship matter in your field?
- What risks concern you: hallucination, privacy, bias, dependency, inequity, environmental impact, academic integrity, deskilling?
- Which of those risks can be mitigated?
- Which risks require a human-only boundary?
- Where might refusing AI exposure itself leave students underprepared?

The objective is not persuasion for its own sake. It is informed agency.

## A simple assignment redesign protocol

### Step 1 — Name the capability
Write one sentence: **After this assignment, students should be better able to…**

### Step 2 — Identify the human core
What cognitive work must the student perform independently?

### Step 3 — Identify possible augmentation
Where could AI improve feedback, iteration, comparison, simulation, accessibility, or exploration?

### Step 4 — Introduce verification
Require students to check claims, sources, calculations, assumptions, or reasoning as appropriate.

### Step 5 — Make process visible
Consider process notes, drafts, decision logs, source trails, oral explanation, demonstrations, or reflections.

### Step 6 — Require authorship
Students should be able to explain what they accepted, rejected, changed, and ultimately believe.

## Faculty principle

> **Do not ask only whether students used AI. Ask what thinking remained theirs.**
